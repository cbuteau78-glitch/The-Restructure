Leverage Toolkit - 12 Forms of Influence

Influence is not a single tool. It is a toolkit. Notice which forms you reach for naturally - and which you avoid. Your gaps are your growth edges.

Hard Power
1. MIGHT
Using authority or consequences to compel action.
Workplace: Setting hard deadlines, exercising formal authority. Effective for compliance, not internalization. Use minimum necessary - power invokes resistance.
Overuse erodes trust and triggers reactance.
2. ETHOS
Credibility from expertise, track record, and character.
Workplace: "I restructured three orgs like this. Here is what worked." Your background is currency - spend it deliberately.
Claimed but undemonstrated ethos backfires.
Smart Power
3. LOGOS
Logical argument, data, evidence.
Workplace: "Shipping speed increased 40% at comparable companies." Works with analytical people. Insufficient alone for relationship-driven people.
4. SITUATION AWARENESS
Reading who matters, what is unsaid, what is actually happening.
Workplace: Noticing the real decision-maker is not in the room. Understanding why someone resists before they tell you.
5. INTENTIONALITY
Being deliberate and strategic - not leaving things to chance.
Workplace: Planning which conversations to have before a big meeting. Designing relationship-building, not assuming it happens.
6. COALITIONS
Building alliances across groups for coordinated influence.
Workplace: You do not need everyone. You need the right people. Three department heads aligned beats ten peers who agree with you.
Your growth area - Leverage Inventory: 3.0/4.0
7. EXCHANGE
Negotiating, trading value, making explicit offers.
Workplace: "I will help with your Q3 analysis if you support this initiative tomorrow." Transactional but powerful. Some people respond to nothing else.
Your growth area - Leverage Inventory: 2.67/4.0
8. AGENCY
Taking initiative. Making things happen without waiting for permission.
Workplace: Solving a problem before being asked. Volunteering for the hard assignment. Shows leadership before the title.
Soft Power
9. PATHOS
Emotional appeals - connecting to what people feel and care about.
Workplace: "This matters because it affects people's ability to do meaningful work." Your strongest natural tool. Not everyone responds to emotion.
Natural strength - Leverage Inventory: 3.67/4.0
10. ALLOCENTRISM
Genuine interest in others - their perspective, needs, and goals.
Workplace: Asking Marcus what he actually cares about before pitching him anything. Designing around real needs, not stated positions.
Natural strength - Leverage Inventory: 3.67/4.0
11. NETWORKS
Building and leveraging relationships across different groups.
Workplace: Knowing who bridges departments informally. Identifying structural holes - who connects what would otherwise be disconnected.
12. TEAM-BUILDING
Deliberately cultivating relationships one by one, over time, with consistency.
Workplace: Scheduling individual coffee chats. Following up. Remembering what people mentioned. This is not networking - it is relationship maintenance.
Your growth area - Leverage Inventory: 2.4/4.0
